## 1. Tweet Hunter Deep Dive

### Growth to ~$8M ARR

**Timeline:**
- **May 2021**: Tweet Hunter founded by Tibo Louis-Lucas and Tom Jacquesson
- **Aug 2021** (~3 months in): Reached $5K MRR from organic Twitter growth
- **Sep 2021**: JK Molina partnership begins; revenue triples from $5K to $15K MRR within 2 weeks
- **Late 2021**: Climbed to $25K MRR
- **May 2022** (12 months): Hit $1M ARR
- **Aug 2022** (15 months): Acquired by Lempire
- **By time Tibo left**: Combined Tweet Hunter + Taplio reached $8M ARR

Tibo stated on X: *"Built Tweet Hunter from scratch, grew to $1m ARR. Then did the same with Taplio. Together they grew to $8m ARR when I left."* ([source](https://x.com/tibo_maker/status/1907778962841059424))

**Revenue at acquisition**: ~$312K MRR combined (Tweet Hunter + Taplio). The deal was $2M upfront + earnout bonuses tied to growth milestones, with total potential payout of $8-10M.

### The Feature That Drove Retention

**The Viral Tweet Library** was Tweet Hunter's killer retention feature. It contained 2M+ searchable viral tweets curated with the help of professional ghostwriters across 10+ topics. Users could:
- Search by topic/niche to find proven tweet formats
- Get AI-generated tweet ideas based on the library
- Access 3,000+ "Staff Picks" handpicked by professionals
- Save tweets into unlimited collections for inspiration

This was combined with **AI-powered writing** (initially GPT-3, later fine-tuned AI21 models) that could generate tweets, rewrite content, finish drafts, and predict tweet performance ("TweetPredict"). The AI21 case study noted the platform became *"10 times faster with the AI integration"* and the fine-tuned model produced *"personalized, highly viral tweet formats."* ([source](https://www.ai21.com/blog/tweet-hunter-case-study/))

The combination of inspiration (library) + execution (AI writing) + automation (scheduling, auto-DMs, auto-plug) created a daily-use habit loop.

### The "Influencer as Co-Founder" Equity Deal

This was Tweet Hunter's breakout GTM innovation. Here's exactly how it worked:

**The Setup**: Tibo and Tom had built Tweet Hunter to ~$5K MRR but had no presence in "Money Twitter" — the community of people monetizing their Twitter audience. JK Molina had ~40K followers deeply embedded in that world, running a coaching business teaching people to grow on Twitter.

**The Deal Structure**: Rather than paying JK an affiliate commission, they gave him **10% equity** in Tweet Hunter (when the company was doing ~$10K MRR). The equity vested based on revenue milestones — he unlocked specific amounts at $10K/month, $15K/month, $25K/month, and so on.

**Why It Worked**:
1. **Alignment**: Equity made JK a true co-founder, not a hired promoter. He was deeply invested in the product's success.
2. **Flywheel**: JK's coaching business + Tweet Hunter created a virtuous cycle. His clients needed a tool; Tweet Hunter was the answer. Tweet Hunter's features made JK's coaching more effective.
3. **Authenticity**: JK wasn't reading ad copy. He was genuinely using and evangelizing the product because he owned a piece of it.
4. **New market access**: Tweet Hunter opened up to an entirely new audience segment overnight.

**Result**: Revenue tripled from $5K to $15K MRR within two weeks of JK's involvement. Within months, it reached $25K MRR.

Sources: [Acquire.com interview](https://blog.acquire.com/startup-acquisition-episode-77/), [Listen Up IH](https://www.listenupih.com/tweethunter/), [Bootstrappers.com](https://bootstrappers.com/these-founders-found-25k-mrr-by-dumbing-down-their-twitter-tool-and-finding-a-spokesperson/)

### Why Tibo Left & What Happened Post-Acquisition

**The Acquisition by Lempire (Jan 2023)**:
- Acquirer: Lempire (parent company of Lemlist), founded by Tibo's middle school friend Guillaume Moubeche
- Deal: $2M cash upfront + earnout up to $6-8M tied to revenue/profitability milestones
- Strategic rationale: Lempire wanted to add social selling tools to their sales automation suite (Lemlist for cold email, Taplio for LinkedIn, Tweet Hunter for Twitter)

**Post-Acquisition: What Went Wrong**

Tibo received ~$6M of the possible $8M earnout, but the experience was deeply negative:

1. **Stressful earnout**: *"Because they wanted a high multiple, they opted for an earnout that was based on performance, requiring very high revenue milestones, which made for a very stressful two years."* The pressure of hitting targets under someone else's ownership was crushing.

2. **Loss of creative control**: Tibo felt *"a deep connection to his creations"* and *"letting them go left a void."*

3. **Product direction concerns**: Tibo expressed frustration that *"he gave up on his baby and now sees the new owners screwing things up."*

4. **Combined products grew but founder was miserable**: Tweet Hunter and Taplio combined grew to $8M ARR under Lempire, proving the earnout-driven growth, but at significant personal cost.

**What Tibo Would Do Differently**:
- **Never do an earnout again**: He explicitly said he won't repeat this structure. The performance pressure detracts from the joy of building.
- **Keep building and own**: His new strategy is building a portfolio of smaller SaaS tools (Revid, Feather, SuperX) and growing them slowly while living off profits.
- **Focus on customers, not contests**: His first two startups won many startup contests but had zero customers. Tweet Hunter had zero contest wins but many customers.
- **Build what you personally use**: Tibo's advantage was being his own target user. He didn't need to do customer research — he was the customer.

Sources: [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/heres-how-tibo-sold-two-products-for-8m-and-why-he-ll-never-do-it-again-iwykhfENoZqJgRLU33zv), [Indie Bites podcast](https://indiebites.com/115), [MoneyMint](https://moneymint.org/p/how-tibo-sold-two-startups-for-8m)

## 2. Breakcold Deep Dive

### Positioning & Product

Breakcold positions itself as "the AI-Native CRM that automates the boring part" — specifically a **Social Selling CRM** that unifies LinkedIn, Twitter/X, and email into one interface. Founded by **Arnaud Belinga** (former tax lawyer) with technical co-founder Mateo.

**Core differentiation**: Unlike traditional CRMs that treat social media as an afterthought, Breakcold makes social engagement the primary workflow:
- See prospects' LinkedIn and Twitter posts directly in the CRM
- Like, comment, and engage without leaving the app
- Track social interactions alongside email conversations
- Auto-score leads based on engagement signals

### What Users Love

From Capterra, G2, and AppSumo reviews:
- **Social feed integration**: *"Breakcold allows me to follow my prospects in separate lists in order to comment on their posts, all in one interface"*
- **LinkedIn prospecting**: Users report *"LinkedIn prospecting efforts seem 10x more effective and professional with Breakcold"*
- **Founder engagement**: *"Positively impressed that Arnaud (founder) meet his customers in 1-1 and ask for feedback, share tips"*
- **Customer support**: Described as *"world-class with spot-on initial training and white glove service"*

### What Users Hate

- **Limited platform support**: Multiple reviewers want more social networks beyond LinkedIn and Twitter
- **Data import limitations**: Inability to import existing data from other CRMs and enrich social profiles
- **Missing features**: No 2-way email sync (eagerly awaited by users)
- **Learning curve**: Takes time to become proficient
- **AppSumo support issues**: Some lifetime deal buyers reported zero response from support and refusal to activate purchased licenses

### Pricing

| Plan | Price | Key Features |
|------|-------|--------------|
| CRM | $29.99/user/mo | Email management, 30-day analytics, unlimited contacts |
| CRM Pro | $59.99/user/mo | Unified inbox, social activity sync, auto lead scoring, email campaigns |
| CRM Max | $79.99/user/mo | Advanced automation workflows, full social sync |

### Business Metrics

- **Revenue (2024)**: ~$43.9K (per Latka data) — still very early stage
- **Team**: Small, bootstrapped
- **Growth channel**: Heavy AppSumo lifetime deal presence for early traction

**Key Takeaway**: Breakcold proves there's demand for social-first CRM, but it's a relationship management tool, not a people *discovery* tool. The gap: you need to already know who your prospects are.

Sources: [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/10011093/Breakcold/reviews/), [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/breakcold), [Breakcold pricing](https://www.breakcold.com/pricing)

## 3. PhantomBuster Deep Dive

### Overview

PhantomBuster is a cloud-based automation platform with 150+ pre-built "Phantoms" (automations) across LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram, Facebook, GitHub, and more. It's positioned as a no-code scraping and automation toolkit for growth hackers and sales teams.

### Twitter/X Workflows People Build

**Key Twitter Phantoms:**

| Phantom | What It Does | Limitations |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Twitter Follower Collector | Extract followers from target accounts | Max 70 most recent followers per account (Twitter restriction) |
| Twitter Profile Scraper | Extract handle, bio, followers, location, website | Requires account cookies |
| Twitter Auto Follow | Automatically follow users from a list | Rate limited |
| Twitter Tweet Extractor | Scrape tweets from users/searches | Subject to API limits |
| Twitter Message Sender | Auto-send DMs to collected users | High ban risk |

**Common Workflow Chains:**
1. **Competitor follower poaching**: Follower Collector -> Profile Scraper -> Auto Follow -> Message Sender
2. **Hashtag lead gen**: Tweet Extractor (search hashtag) -> Profile Scraper -> Export to CRM
3. **Influencer outreach**: Profile Scraper -> Enrich with email -> Send via Lemlist/Clay

### User Experience: The Good and Bad

**Positive (G2: 4.4/5, Capterra: 4.5/5):**
- Quick setup: single Phantom operational in under 5 minutes
- Good for LinkedIn automation specifically
- Zapier/n8n integrations for complex workflows

**Negative — critical issues:**
- **Scripts break constantly**: *"Phantoms break when platforms update their interfaces, requiring constant monitoring"*
- **Poor documentation**: *"Documentation is described as terrible, with poor API documentation"*
- **Expensive for what you get**: Most common complaint is cost vs. value
- **Support quality varies**: One user spending $1,000/month reported *"very slow support, with no response for over 20 days"*
- **Account safety risks**: Users report LinkedIn/Twitter accounts getting restricted after heavy usage
- **Twitter limitations are severe**: Only 70 followers per account extraction. Compare this to LinkedIn where you can extract thousands.

### Pricing

| Plan | Monthly | Annual |
|------|---------|--------|
| Free | $0 (very limited) | - |
| Starter | $59/mo | $48/mo |
| Pro | $139/mo | $112/mo |
| Team | $399/mo | $320/mo |

Usage-based model combining slots, execution time, and credits. A fast Phantom can enrich 200-300 profiles/hour.

**Key Takeaway**: PhantomBuster is powerful for LinkedIn but severely limited on Twitter/X. The 70-follower extraction cap makes Twitter workflows nearly useless at scale. It's a Swiss Army knife, not a purpose-built people discovery tool.

Sources: [PhantomBuster support docs](https://support.phantombuster.com/), [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/173165/Phantombuster/reviews/), [Salesforge overview](https://www.salesforge.ai/directory/sales-tools/phantombuster)

## 4. SparkToro Deep Dive

### How SparkToro Thinks About "Finding People"

SparkToro's fundamental insight is that **you shouldn't find individual people — you should understand audiences**. Rather than giving you a list of Twitter handles to DM, SparkToro answers: *"Where does my target audience hang out online, and what do they pay attention to?"*

**Their approach:**
1. Crawl millions of social profiles, websites, podcasts, and online platforms
2. Pull data from 11 social networks plus tens of millions of websites
3. Index bio attributes, following data, shares, and engagement patterns
4. Let users query in natural language: *"CEOs at SaaS companies in the US"*
5. Return aggregate insights: demographics, websites they visit, accounts they follow, podcasts they listen to, hashtags they use

**What SparkToro tells you (that others don't):**
- Which podcasts your audience listens to (great for sponsorship)
- Which YouTube channels they watch
- Which websites/blogs they read
- Which social accounts they follow
- Demographic breakdowns: gender, age range, geography, job titles

### Rand Fishkin's "Chill Work" Philosophy

SparkToro is intentionally not a venture-backed hypergrowth startup. Fishkin calls it "chill work":
- **Team**: Just 3 employees total
- **Revenue**: ~$330K (per Latka data, 2025)
- **Work hours**: Never demands more than 30 hours/week
- **Funding**: Middle ground between bootstrapped and VC — investors get dividends from profits, not a liquidity event

Fishkin's pitch to investors: *"We need to build a small, few-million-dollars-a-year profitable business within 5-10 years to return investor money and generate dividends."*

### Pricing (Updated 2025)

SparkToro moved to unlimited searches for paid plans:

| Plan | Price | Key Features |
|------|-------|--------------|
| Free | $0 | 5-10 searches/month, sample results only |
| Basic | $150/mo | Unlimited searches, full results |
| Premium | $300/mo | Advanced features, team access |
| Unlimited | $600/mo | Enterprise features |

### User Complaints

- **Missing demographic data**: Doesn't provide traditional age/gender breakdowns
- **Aggregate, not individual**: You get audience profiles, not actionable lists of people to contact
- **Search-based pricing felt limiting** (before the switch to unlimited)
- **Data freshness**: Crawled data may be weeks/months old

**Key Takeaway**: SparkToro answers "where is my audience?" but not "who specifically should I talk to?" It's a media buying and content strategy tool, not a networking or outreach tool. There's a massive gap between "your audience reads TechCrunch" and "here are 15 people you should connect with and why."

Sources: [SparkToro pricing](https://sparktoro.com/pricing), [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/sparktoro), [dot.la interview](https://dot.la/rand-fishkin-sparktoro-2658499274.html), [SparkToro Year 2 Retrospective](https://sparktoro.com/blog/sparktoros-year-2-retrospective-can-chill-work-alternative-funding-and-an-indie-approach-scale/)

## 5. Winning GTM Playbooks

### How Successful Twitter Tools Acquired First Users

#### Pattern 1: Build in Public on Twitter (Tweet Hunter, Typefully, Hypefury)

The single most effective GTM channel for Twitter tools is... Twitter itself. This creates a natural flywheel:

**Tweet Hunter's playbook:**
1. Tibo started with zero followers. Built Tweet Hunter in public on Twitter.
2. The act of building the tool in public attracted the exact target audience.
3. Shared revenue milestones, feature updates, and learnings daily.
4. Launched tiny side projects around the same niche monthly, each getting a Product Hunt launch that drove eyeballs back to Tweet Hunter.
5. Each Product Hunt launch brought awareness; each BIP tweet converted followers.

Key quote from analysis: *"Building in Public doesn't work for all products, but it is the perfect growth strategy for a Twitter scheduling tool because BIP is part of Twitter culture."*

**Hypefury's playbook:**
1. Samy Dindane tweeted asking if anyone needed a thread scheduling tool
2. Got 10-20 responses, started a private beta with those people
3. Did 1:1 demos with every early user
4. Each user introduced 2-3 more ideal users (organic referral)
5. No paid ads, no big PH launch initially — just Twitter + Indie Hackers communities
6. Grew from $300 to $18K MRR in 15 months purely through word-of-mouth

**Typefully's playbook:**
1. Built a tiny side project to preview Twitter threads (their "itch")
2. It went viral on Hacker News
3. Hit $5K MRR within days
4. Grew from there to 150K users and $1.6M ARR
5. Team of fewer than 10 people, fully bootstrapped

#### Pattern 2: Influencer Equity Partnerships (Tweet Hunter)

As detailed above, giving JK Molina 10% equity was dramatically more effective than any paid marketing. Key learning: **equity-aligned influencers outperform hired promoters by 10x** because they become genuine advocates.

#### Pattern 3: AppSumo Lifetime Deals (Breakcold, various)

Works as a launchpad for early traction + feedback, but has trade-offs:
- **Pros**: Immediate revenue, customer feedback, community building
- **Cons**: Supporting lifetime users forever, AppSumo takes 30-70% cut, investors don't value LTD revenue

#### Pattern 4: Niche-Down Aggressively (Hypefury)

Hypefury succeeded in a crowded market by being the first tool that could schedule Twitter threads — nobody else did it. This hyper-specific feature attracted a specific audience, then they expanded.

#### Channel Effectiveness Ranking (for Twitter tools specifically):

1. **Building in public on Twitter** — free, high-intent audience, natural flywheel
2. **Influencer equity partnerships** — 10x more effective than paid sponsorships
3. **Product Hunt launches** — good for spikes, especially when combined with BIP
4. **Indie Hackers / Hacker News** — developer/maker community, high-quality early users
5. **AppSumo** — good for cash injection, risky for long-term unit economics
6. **Paid ads** — generally NOT effective for these tools (too niche, Twitter audience is ad-skeptical)

Sources: [Baremetrics - Hypefury Growth](https://baremetrics.com/blog/hypefury-growth), [Superframeworks - TweetHunter](https://superframeworks.com/blog/tweethunter), [Indie Hackers - Tweet Hunter AMA](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-tweet-hunters-mvp-in-2-weeks-4-months-later-it-grew-to-150k-arr-ama-a6ae1ee850), [Francesco Di Lorenzo - Typefully Launch](https://francescodilorenzo.com/typefully-launch)

## 6. Failed Tools & Lessons

### The Twitter API Apocalypse (Feb 2023)

When Elon Musk eliminated free API access in February 2023, it triggered a mass extinction event:

**Tools that shut down or were forced to sell:**

| Tool | What Happened | Lesson |
|------|--------------|--------|
| **Zlappo** | Shut down entirely (May 2023). Couldn't afford new API pricing. Told users to "bring your own API key" which violates Twitter ToS. | Complete platform dependency = existential risk |
| **BlackMagic.so** (Tony Dinh) | Sold for $128K despite doing $14K MRR ($168K ARR). New API pricing ($42K/month minimum) made it unprofitable overnight. | Even profitable businesses can be killed by platform changes |
| **Crowdfire** | Shut down after 15 years. Twitter API became unaffordable for free/low-tier users. | Long-running businesses aren't immune to platform risk |
| **TweetDeck** | Locked behind Twitter/X Premium paywall. URL changed from tweetdeck.twitter.com to pro.x.com. | Twitter itself cannibalized its ecosystem |

**Tools that survived the API changes:**

| Tool | How They Survived |
|------|------------------|
| **Tweet Hunter** | Already acquired by Lempire with resources to absorb costs |
| **Hypefury** | Used the API price hike to **acquire competitors** who couldn't afford it ([Paddle webinar](https://www.paddle.com/events/webinars/growth-by-acquisition-hypefury-twitters-price-hike)) |
| **Typefully** | Had enough revenue ($1M+ ARR) to absorb the cost |

### Tony Dinh / BlackMagic.so — Detailed Case Study

Tony Dinh built BlackMagic.so (Twitter analytics + CRM) from $0 to $14K MRR as a solo founder. The tool used Twitter's API heavily. When the new pricing was announced at a minimum of $42K/month:

- The entire product ran on the Twitter API and could go down anytime
- He sold for $128K — a fraction of the ~$168K ARR it was generating
- He pivoted to building Xnapper (screenshot tool) and TypingMind (ChatGPT interface) — neither dependent on a single platform's API

**Key lesson**: *"The only way for the product to continue to exist was to sell it."* Platform dependency is not a theoretical risk — it's an existential one.

### Broader Failure Patterns

1. **Over-reliance on official Twitter API**: Tools that used the official API were most vulnerable. Those using unofficial scraping methods (like SocialData.tools) were less affected but face ToS risk.

2. **No moat beyond the API**: Tools that were thin wrappers around Twitter's API had no defensibility. When the API got expensive, they had nothing to fall back on.

3. **Free-tier dependency**: Tools that served primarily free users couldn't raise prices enough to cover new API costs.

4. **Single-platform risk**: Every tool that died was 100% dependent on Twitter. Multi-platform tools (like later Typefully adding LinkedIn) had more resilience.

Sources: [TechCrunch - Twitter API](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/01/twitter-to-end-free-access-to-its-api/), [Tony Dinh newsletter](https://news.tonydinh.com/p/may-2023-i-sold-my-2-years-old-business), [Hypefury - Zlappo shutdown](https://hypefury.com/zlappo/), [SocialBee - Crowdfire alternatives](https://socialbee.com/blog/crowdfire-alternatives/)

## 7. The Gap: What LLM-Powered Discovery Can Do Differently

### What Existing Tools Do (and Don't Do)

| Tool | Finds People? | Understands Intent? | Explains "Why"? | Real-time? |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Tweet Hunter | No (content creation tool) | No | No | N/A |
| Breakcold | No (manages known prospects) | No | No | N/A |
| PhantomBuster | Sort of (scrapes followers) | No | No | Batch |
| SparkToro | Audiences, not individuals | Partially (queries) | No | Crawled data |
| Twitter Advanced Search | Yes, but crude | No | No | Yes |
| Fedica/Circleboom | Bio search only | No | No | Partial |
| Clay | Enriches known leads | No | No | Batch |

### The Fundamental Gaps

**Gap 1: Intent-Driven Discovery**

No existing tool lets you say *"I'm building a developer tool for API testing and want to find DevRel professionals who are actively talking about API pain points"* and get back a curated, ranked list of people. Current tools require you to already know:
- Which keywords to search
- Which accounts to scrape followers from
- Which hashtags to monitor

An LLM can translate vague human intent into multiple search strategies, then judge relevance with human-like understanding.

**Gap 2: Multi-Query Intelligence**

Twitter's search is keyword-based. A single query misses most relevant people. An LLM can generate 5-7 semantically different queries that approach the same intent from different angles:
- Direct keyword matches
- Adjacent topic conversations
- Community-specific language
- Problem-based discussions
- Tool/product mentions

This dramatically increases recall while the LLM's ranking maintains precision.

**Gap 3: Contextual Relevance Scoring**

PhantomBuster gives you a list of 70 followers. Twitter search gives you tweets. Neither tells you *why* a person is relevant to your specific goal. An LLM can:
- Read someone's tweets and bio
- Understand the searcher's intent
- Write a personalized explanation of why this person matters
- Score relevance on a nuanced scale (not just keyword matching)

**Gap 4: The "Cold Start" Problem**

Every existing tool assumes you already know something:
- **Breakcold**: You know your prospects (it's a CRM)
- **PhantomBuster**: You know which accounts to scrape
- **SparkToro**: You know your audience descriptor
- **Clay**: You have a lead list to enrich

An LLM-powered discovery tool solves the cold start: *"I just started a company and don't know anyone in this space. Who should I be talking to?"*

**Gap 5: Explanation & Actionability**

SparkToro tells you "your audience follows @naval." So what? An LLM can tell you: *"@jane_devrel works at Stripe, has been tweeting about API testing frustrations for 3 months, recently asked for tool recommendations, and has 15K engaged followers in the developer community. She'd be a great early adopter AND potential champion."*

**Gap 6: Real-Time vs. Crawled**

SparkToro relies on crawled data that can be weeks or months old. An LLM + live Twitter search finds people based on what they're saying *right now*.

### What You Can Build That Nobody Else Has

**The "Find My People" Tool:**
1. **Natural language input**: Describe who you want to find and why, in plain English
2. **LLM generates search strategy**: Multiple queries from different angles
3. **Live Twitter search**: Finds real, recent conversations
4. **LLM ranks & explains**: Every result has a personalized "why this person" explanation
5. **Quality assessment**: Not just a dump of results — tells you if the results are strong or if you should refine

**Potential differentiators:**
- **No learning curve**: Unlike PhantomBuster's 150 Phantoms or SparkToro's query language
- **Individual people, not audiences**: Unlike SparkToro's aggregate approach
- **Intent-aware**: Unlike keyword-matching tools
- **Real-time**: Unlike crawled databases
- **Explainable**: Every recommendation has reasoning

### Competitive Moat Considerations

**What's defensible:**
- Prompt engineering and ranking algorithms (harder to copy than features)
- Data from multiple search strategies (compounds over time)
- User feedback loop (which results did users find useful? Improves ranking)

**What's NOT defensible:**
- Twitter API access (anyone can use SocialData.tools or similar)
- LLM capabilities (anyone can call Claude/GPT)
- The basic idea (someone can clone the concept)

**True moat**: The quality of the search-to-ranking pipeline. This is an AI-native product where the intelligence IS the product, not a feature bolted on.

### Market Positioning Opportunity

The competitive landscape has a clear pattern:

```
Content Creation Tools          CRM / Outreach Tools         Intelligence Tools
(Tweet Hunter, Typefully,       (Breakcold, Clay,           (SparkToro, Audiense,
 Hypefury)                       Lemlist)                    Fedica)
       |                              |                           |
       v                              v                           v
  "Help me post"              "Help me manage"            "Help me understand"
```

**What's missing:**

```
                         Discovery Tools
                    "Help me FIND the right people"
                              |
                              v
               (Your tool goes here)
```

No tool in the market today does discovery well. They all assume you've already found your people. This is the gap.
